# Problem Statement

## Background

Waste management is one of the most pressing challenges in urban sustainability. Municipal recycling programmes depend entirely on citizens correctly segregating waste at the point of disposal — yet the guidance available to the average person is vague, inconsistent, and not actionable in the moment. Recycling bin labels show broad categories like "Plastics" without distinguishing between the seven fundamentally different resin types, each of which has a completely different recyclability profile, market value, and contamination risk.

---

## The Problem

**Wish-cycling** — the habit of tossing questionable items into recycling bins in the hope they will be recycled — is responsible for an estimated 25% contamination rate in municipal recycling streams across urban India and globally. When a non-recyclable item contaminates a recycling batch, the entire batch may be diverted to landfill, negating the efforts of every other citizen who disposed correctly.

At the same time, consumers face two concrete knowledge gaps at the exact moment they are holding a piece of waste:

1. **Category identification** — "Is this Glass? Metal? Miscellaneous Trash?" Without real-time visual AI assistance, this is often guesswork.
2. **Plastic resin identification** — Even within the Plastic category, a PET (#1) bottle is fully recyclable in most cities, a PVC (#3) pipe is chemically hazardous to recycle streams, and PS (#6) foam is almost universally rejected. The Resin Identification Code (RIC) stamped on packaging is tiny, inconsistently placed, and unknown to the majority of consumers.

On the municipal side, city planners and waste management authorities have no real-time visibility into *what* citizens are disposing, *where*, and with what error rate — making it impossible to target public education campaigns or optimise collection routes.

---

## Who Is Affected

| Stakeholder | Pain Point |
|---|---|
| **Urban citizens** | No point-of-disposal guidance; uncertain whether an item is recyclable; no feedback loop reinforcing correct behaviour |
| **Municipal waste authorities** | No granular, real-time data on waste composition or contamination hotspots |
| **Recycling facilities** | High contamination in incoming streams drives up sorting costs and causes batch rejections |
| **Environment** | Recyclable materials that could re-enter the circular economy end up in landfills, generating unnecessary CO₂ and methane |

The problem is acute for the **18–35 urban demographic** that is willing to recycle but lacks the knowledge infrastructure to do so correctly, and for **tier-1 and tier-2 Indian cities** scaling their recycling programmes without the public education budgets of developed nations.

---

## Why It Matters

- India generates approximately **62 million tonnes** of solid waste annually, of which less than 20% is processed scientifically.
- Plastic contamination in recycling streams costs municipal corporations significant remediation expense per tonne to sort.
- Every kilogram of aluminium correctly recycled saves **9 kg of CO₂** equivalent versus virgin production; glass saves **0.3 kg CO₂/kg**; cardboard saves **~1 kg CO₂/kg**.
- Behavioural research consistently shows that **immediate, personalised feedback** is the single most effective mechanism for changing disposal habits — yet no scalable, real-time solution exists for the average citizen.

---

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

| Existing Approach | Limitation |
|---|---|
| Static bin labels ("Plastics / Paper / Wet Waste") | No sub-category guidance; no way to identify resin codes or borderline items |
| Municipal awareness pamphlets and posters | Not available at the moment of disposal; quickly forgotten |
| General-purpose recycling websites / apps | Require manual text search; no image-based identification; no personalisation |
| Industrial sorting conveyors (NIR, optical) | Operate at the facility level after collection — too late to correct citizen behaviour or provide educational feedback |
| Smart bin pilots (weight sensors, fill-level) | Measure *quantity*, not *type* — provide no guidance to the individual depositor |

None of these solutions address the core gap: **an intelligent, real-time, image-driven assistant that meets citizens at the point of disposal, tells them exactly what to do, explains why, and reinforces positive behaviour over time.**
